# Principles of Application and Study

This document establishes the scientific and legal guidelines that underpin the research and methodological application of the project, aiming to ensure maximum legal certainty, statistical rigor, and analytical reproducibility.

## I. Legal-Institutional Axis

1. **Normative Exhaustiveness:** The analysis of the statistical and population universe of roles and attributions must consider the entirety of the normative framework (laws, decrees, ordinances, public notices, and jurisprudence). Arbitrary selectivity or the exclusion of any legal instrument that generates competencies is prohibited.

2. **Prohibition of Deviant Extensive Exegesis:** The norms defining attributions must be interpreted with strict hermeneutic rigor. It is forbidden to distort, expand, ignore, or minimize the scope of a norm to favor biased results. Statistical precision demands respect for normative completeness and literality.

3. **Historical-Evolutionary Perspective and Administrative Praxis:** The genesis and organic-institutional evolution of roles must be considered. Administrative praxis, institutional customs, and documentary evidence serve as subsidiary elements for understanding the factual reality (organic evolution), provided, however, they align with the precepts of legality and consolidated jurisprudence.

4. **Federative Harmonization and Standardization:** The peculiarities and regionalisms of the State of São Paulo must invariably be cross-referenced with national guidelines (e.g., the National Organic Law of Civil Polices) as well as national and international doctrine, seeking standardization and alignment with global trends in forensic and police sciences.

5. **Motivation of the Administrative Act of Reclassification:** Any proposal for renaming roles or restructuring attributions must be strictly motivated (presuppositions of fact and law). The motivation must prove the fulfillment of public need and the principles of Public Administration, rejecting concessions based on corporatism or punctual class gains.

6. **Supremacy of Public Interest and Impersonality:** Proposals for insertion, suppression, or modification in databases or organic structures must be exclusively guided by the pursuit of efficiency and improvement of the Public Service. Methodological decisions must not have a tendency to favor classes, always prioritizing the social interest.

7. **Prevalence of Specialty:** In an eventual apparent conflict of norms or functions, the specialty (investigative, notarial, assistance, or forensic) must be weighed against the general norm (*Lex specialis derogat legi generali*).

## II. Methodological and Analytical Axis

8. **Technical Rigor and Objective Parameterization:** The technique inherent to each role must be analyzed with inflexible mathematical and scientific rigor. Comparisons and similarity assessments must be based on objective and quantifiable metrics (e.g., vectorization of variables, conversion into binary presence/absence tables).

9. **Algorithmic Transparency and Reproducibility (White-Box Model):** The study's methodology must be sound, understandable, and documented, allowing full reproducibility. The generated calculations and data extraction logic must be auditable, ensuring that the results and decisions derived from them possess irrefutable scientific backing.

10. **Census Analysis and Bias Mitigation:** The study must, at all times and whenever feasible, seek to cover the entire "statistical population" of roles and attributions under scrutiny. Sample or analytical bias must be peremptorily avoided; if its total mitigation is unfeasible, the remaining bias must be explicitly warned and justified.

11. **Data Integrity and Computational Efficiency:** Raw data must be subjected to rigorous processes of sanitization, modeling, and processing. The extensive use of computational resources and techniques (Data Science) is imperative to ensure efficiency, scalability of calculations, and the clarity of the presentation of results.

## III. Visualization and Cognitive Heuristics Axis

12. **Visual Communication and Heuristic Entropy:** Mathematical findings must be translated into accessible visual representations (dendrograms, graphs, heat maps, etc.). The controlled introduction of noise in the data (entropy) and the exploration of artistic representations of a subsidiary nature are admitted, not as a bias, but as a heuristic tool to evoke new cognitive perceptions ("emotion over noise") and break interpretative stagnations regarding complex institutional phenomena.

13. **Epistemic Promotion and Multidisciplinary Diffusion:** The study must transcend mere administrative cataloging, acting as a vector for knowledge creation and disciplinary intersection (Law, Data Science, Public Administration, and Forensic Sciences). The findings and methodologies must prioritize transparency and accessibility, promoting scientific and educational dissemination, in order to equip society, academia, and public managers with structured data for strategic decision-making.
